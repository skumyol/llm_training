Deeper Experiment Plan for Structured NPC Dialogue

The following plan outlines deeper improvements to the structured state pipeline for NPC dialogue, expanding beyond the submission-focused additions. These experiments are intended for a subsequent phase of research and are more ambitious in scope.

A. Multi-Turn Contextual State Modelling

A key limitation of the current pipeline is that each head predicts its labels from a single-turn context, despite relational factors such as familiarity, dominance and trust depending on the dialogue history. Multi-turn modelling addresses this by encoding and updating the social state across several turns.

Data Requirements

  • Collect or generate multi-turn conversations (3–5 turns) where the NPC interacts with the player over time.
  • Label the relational features (familiarity, dominance, trust, secrecy pressure) after each turn along with the correct reveal decision.
  • Include episodes where trust builds slowly and then players attempt to extract secrets, capturing dynamic changes in the state.

Model Architecture

  • Replace the single-turn encoder with a recurrent model (e.g., GRU or Transformer encoder) that processes sequences of NPC–player turns.
  • Maintain a latent state representing the current social relationship; update this state after each turn.
  • Use the latent state as input to each head (response policy, reveal decision, relational deltas).
  • Investigate gating mechanisms to control how much new evidence updates the social state.

Evaluation

  • Compare the multi-turn model to the single-turn baseline on relational delta prediction accuracy.
  • Assess whether reveal decisions and routing become more reliable when conditioned on the updated state.
  • Analyse whether multi-turn encoding reduces spurious familiarity/dominance fluctuations between dialogue turns.

B. Focal Loss & Curriculum Training

Rare events such as secret disclosure and high pressure states are under-represented in the data. Focal loss and curriculum learning can mitigate class imbalance and improve robustness on hard cases.

Focal Loss

  • Incorporate the focal loss with tunable parameter \(\gamma\) for the reveal decision and secrecy pressure heads.
  • Tune \(\gamma\) to emphasize misclassified minority examples; start with values in {1, 2, 5}.
  • Monitor whether focal loss improves recall for rare events without significantly degrading precision.

Curriculum Learning

  • Organize training episodes by difficulty—start with in-distribution, single-intent interactions, then gradually introduce adversarial manipulations (authority mimicry, bribery, emotional blackmail).
  • Evaluate whether gradually increasing difficulty leads to more stable training and better performance on hard test cases.
  • Experiment with different curriculum schedules (e.g., linear warm-up, exponential mixture) and measure convergence speed.

C. Contrastive Loss for Relational Deltas

The relational delta heads (familiarity and dominance changes) sometimes learn spurious correlations. A contrastive objective can encourage the model to learn meaningful representations by bringing contexts with similar relational changes closer in embedding space and pushing apart dissimilar contexts.

Loss Formulation

  • Create positive pairs by sampling two contexts whose gold relational deltas are identical (e.g., both reflect an increase in familiarity).
  • Create negative pairs by sampling contexts with different relational deltas.
  • Use an InfoNCE or triplet loss on the embeddings just before the classification layer.
  • Combine the contrastive loss with the classification loss to jointly optimize accuracy and representation quality.

Evaluation

  • Report classification accuracy for relational delta prediction with and without contrastive loss.
  • Perform dimensionality reduction (e.g., t-SNE) on the learned embeddings to visualize clustering by relational delta.
  • Measure cluster cohesion and separation metrics to quantify whether similar deltas group together more tightly.

D. Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for Response Generation

The current generator relies on deterministic routing and sampling from a base language model. To produce more natural and ethically aligned responses, fine-tune the generator using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), which directly optimizes for preferred responses without reward models.

Data Collection

  • Gather pairwise human preferences on generated responses: ask annotators to choose the better response between baseline and alternative outputs for a given context.
  • Label ties when neither response is preferred.
  • Ensure the dataset includes examples across different manipulation types and NPC roles.

DPO Training

  • Use the methodology of DPO: compute the probability ratio between preferred and dispreferred responses according to the base model.
  • Minimize the negative log of the preference-weighted ratios to adjust the generator's parameters.
  • Start with small learning rates to avoid catastrophic forgetting and fine-tune for a few epochs.

Evaluation

  • Compare generation quality metrics (BLEU, ROUGE, Distinct-n, perplexity) before and after DPO.
  • Conduct a human evaluation to verify whether DPO-trained responses are preferred over baseline in terms of believability, secrecy maintenance and emotional appropriateness.
  • Check that DPO training does not increase leakage—combine with the leakage classifier from earlier.

E. Cross-Domain Generalisation Experiments

To demonstrate that the structured state approach is not limited to a single fantasy world, conduct experiments on additional domains (e.g., science fiction, historical, modern settings). This tests whether the heads and router generalize across NPC profiles and world dynamics.

Domain Selection & Data

  • Define 2–3 new game domains with distinct lore and social dynamics, such as a cyberpunk city or medieval court.
  • Generate new dialogue corpora per domain, ensuring each includes secrets, roles and relational dynamics.
  • Label the same latent heads (response policy, reveal decision, relational deltas) on these new corpora.

Transfer Strategies

  • Train the model on the original domain and evaluate zero-shot performance on new domains to measure direct transfer.
  • Fine-tune the model on small amounts of new-domain data to test few-shot adaptability.
  • Explore domain-adaptive pretraining: continue language model pretraining on domain-specific corpora before fine-tuning the heads.

Evaluation Metrics

  • Report cross-domain accuracy and F1 for each head separately.
  • Measure cross-domain gating decisions and leakage rates.
  • Analyse qualitative differences in errors across domains to identify domain-specific challenges.

F. Additional Analyses and Metrics

Beyond the core experiments, further analyses can provide deeper insights into model behaviour and fairness.

Calibration Under Adversarial Conditions

  • Measure calibration error (ECE) on adversarial test sets to see if the model remains reliable under manipulative conditions.
  • Compute coverage vs. accuracy curves for selective routing when adversarial examples are prevalent.

Fairness Across NPC Profiles

  • Check whether certain NPC archetypes (e.g., merchant vs. guard) experience higher leakage or misclassification rates.
  • If disparities exist, investigate adjusting training data or loss weighting to mitigate them.

Robustness to Prompt Variations

  • Test the model on paraphrased or off-distribution player utterances to ensure robustness to linguistic diversity.
  • Include morphological variants, synonyms and colloquial phrases in adversarial sets.

Overall Timeline

Given the increased scope, a staged approach is recommended. An approximate timeline might be:

  • Months 1–2: Multi-turn contextual modelling dataset creation, model development and evaluation.
  • Months 3–4: Implement focal loss, curriculum training and contrastive loss; perform ablation studies.
  • Months 5–6: Collect preference data and apply DPO fine-tuning; run comprehensive human evaluations.
  • Months 7–8: Prepare new domains and run cross-domain transfer experiments; analyse fairness and robustness metrics.
  • Month 9: Consolidate results, draw cross-cutting insights and draft the next paper.

These deeper experiments expand on the submission-focused improvements by tackling long-term challenges in structured NPC dialogue: modelling temporal context, addressing data imbalance, learning richer relational representations, aligning generation with human preferences, and demonstrating domain transferability. They would form the foundation for a more comprehensive research paper or follow-up publication.
